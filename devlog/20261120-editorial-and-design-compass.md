# Horizons ’77: editorial and design compass

Written and revised 20 November 2026 after the ten-copy digest proof and the subsequent three-feature pass exposed a loss of visual character. This is the current guide for further design work. It supersedes the assumption in the October digest notes that 48 pages and a common article template are constraints. The later contributor pass also supersedes the premise of a strong single art editor. It does not supersede the writing, the lived origins, or the friendships that developed through the work.

The [26 November follow-up](20261126-assembled-pages-and-conference-chronology.md)
adds a conference file written strictly from 1977 and updates the digest to 70 pages; earlier feature folios remain unchanged.

Current implementation and visual evidence: [spreads as worlds](20261120-spreads-as-worlds/README.md). The contributed-page and three-feature snapshots preserve intermediate stages.

## What the object is

A proceedings assembled at the close of an imaginary international conference on Tenerife in 1977. The delegates look fifty years ahead, to 2027. Its main form is a 5.5 × 8.5-inch portrait digest; the website carries the magazine's compositions and reading order, with its own folios. The reader should encounter an earnest object from another possible history. Fiction, modern authorship and sources are explained only at the end. All preceding pages speak from inside 1977. For forthcoming real events, establish that they were already being planned by then; never import their eventual outcomes into a delegate’s knowledge.

It is also a totem to friendships, new and old. The personal recollections are not intrusions into an architectural magazine: they are part of why these proposals matter. A pair of minds may see something neither could see alone. Keep their differences, their arguments and the particular things they do together. Do not resolve this into repeated declarations about the importance of friendship.

## We side with the dreamers

The humour has pathos. Time will undo much of what the delegates build; they nevertheless build. Their elegant plans are surrounded by unruly, fecund life, by interruptions, repairs, heat, appetite, money, fatigue and mortality. The work can expose a pretension without despising the person who needs it. Magic and serendipity are possible within a world that exceeds every scheme.

Anselm's references here are Wes Anderson, Terry Gilliam, Cervantes and Robert Crumb: sincerity, elaborate systems, human excess, quixotic persistence. These are an editorial compass, not an instruction to imitate a filmmaker's palette or a cartoonist's hand. Anderson is not a reference available to the 1977 editorial office. Gilliam is the young filmmaker of *Jabberwocky*, not the later maker of *Brazil*. Let hindsight belong to the reader.

Avoid both the flawless future and the smug retrospective. A weather cancellation, an unclaimed cup, two people catching a drawing in the wind can carry more of the tone than an explicit joke. A room's inhabitants should be allowed to exceed its designer's intention. Nature has its own lives and claims; it is not raw material for human optimisation.

## Evidence, not a generic “seventies” costume

The [1 June 1976 issue of *Jericho*](https://habitat.scarp.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Jericho_Issue2_01061976.pdf), preserved by UBC's Habitat Archive, is a particularly useful primary reference. Its first page juxtaposes a large display headline, a large photograph, narrow reporting columns, small notices and a different emphatic secondary headline. Its conspicuous type contrasts matter more than the yellowing of the surviving scan. The [Habitat ’76 archive](https://habitat76.ca/) gives the conference context. We do not represent Tenerife as an actual sequel, or reproduce these pages as our own.

[Archigram's magazine and ephemera collection at Harvard](https://hollisarchives.lib.harvard.edu/catalog/des00046) is a research lead for speculative publishing and differentiated paper objects. Inspect the actual relevant issues before borrowing a particular layout. Do not infer one uniform period style from a catalogue description.

[ITC's Avant Garde field guide](https://www.myfonts.com/a/font/content/font-field-guide/itc-avant-garde-gothic/) dates the typeface's formation to 1970; [Trade Gothic's publisher catalogue](https://www.myfonts.com/pages/fontshop-fontlists-trade-gothic/) identifies Jackson Burke's 1948 family. Both traditions are available to a 1977 editor. They perform different jobs. Availability does not justify using one face on every page.

## Several hands, several kinds of page

The contributions should look as though different groups brought their own sheets. The office gathers them without making them all behave alike. A common trim, readable text, accurate folios and careful binding are enough shared discipline. Do not use a universal TITLE → deck → horizontal drawing → two columns sequence.

- The French forms-of-life group brings an ochre/ultramarine photomontage crossing the gutter (digest 6–7), followed by its remaining argument on page 8. Its image is a metaphor, not a photograph of a real village or a tropical reef.
- The excursion notebook brings a dark halftone photographic plate facing a quiet German essay (12–13).
- The secretariat brings narrow reports, heavy condensed mastheads, boxed notices and ordinary accounts. It can disagree typographically with the excursion notebook.
- Energy arrives as a yellow proposition; finance as a subscription prospectus with a restrained red frame. Their words remain the original essays, not advertising filler.
- The film desk brings an overflowing ink castle across 50–51, followed by the richer Gilliam writing. The people and plants exceed the apparatus. The drawing belongs to the fictional film desk, not to Gilliam's authenticated oeuvre.
- Ocean (14–17) now opens as a continuous inhabited scene with shaped text. Hillside (28–31) and flight (42–45) retain their individually drawn feature compositions.
- Alexander begins with a village stair across 32–33, then unfolds across four reading pages (34–37). A drawing can provide the shape of a reading path.
- The architectural gathering crosses 54–55; Fuller/Snelson and the translation table follow on 56–57. A group picture is a fictional illustrative reconstruction, not documentary evidence.
- The final unsent letters have their own leaf (63). Poetry and Arabic are writing, not decorative filler.

## Type is a voice, not a decade filter

Nimbus Roman in mixed case, bold and italic replaces the geometric Gothic default for most interior headings. Condensed Nimbus Sans supplies the office and engineering voices. Bookman gives the film desk a slightly awkward, heavier literary heading. Mono belongs to annotations and accounts. Amiri supplies the Arabic. Gothic remains in the existing cover and viewer; it is no longer the answer to every interior title.

The sharper letters matter, but a historically available face can still look generic when every page deploys it in the same way. Change scale, measure, position, case and relationship to pictures with purpose. One enormous word, an intimate italic heading and a newspaper masthead can coexist. Do not make every contribution equally loud. Do not add random rotations to compensate for an unchanged underlying template.

Reading type in the contributor compositor is fixed at 9–10.4 pt, generally two columns with natural spacing. When a page does not fit, reconsider the distribution before reducing type. The English opening and Drago gained room; the closing letters gained a leaf. Captions and smaller legacy notes need a physical 100% proof on the intended stock.

## Compose the binding

Even folios are left-hand pages, odd folios right-hand pages. Cross-gutter artwork is one continuous composition; live text stays clear of the glue margin. Keep text as real type over artwork. Do not rasterise full essays into an image-generator facsimile. On small screens the two halves run sequentially and their paragraphs leave the image for a separate reading area.

White space can be a deliberate change of pace. It should not be what remains after mechanically halving a paragraph array. Do not generate additional generic prose to fill holes. Preserve rich arguments, personal recollections and the difficult friendships; retire redundant pictures first.

## Pictures must earn their place

Commission a specific plate for a specific proposition. Vary media: the ocean is a painted architectural perspective, the hillside a ruled sectional drawing with sparse colour, the lift station a strongly separated industrial illustration. The technical studies remain subordinate explanatory documents; they cannot stand in for every imagined world.

Never repeat a primary picture to fill space. A return to a detail must introduce a different reading, be explicitly captioned as a detail and be deliberately composed. Preserve original assets; do not overwrite them when revising. Keep image text out of generated art wherever possible; headlines and captions must be editable, searchable type.

Tenerife matters: Teide's presence, volcanic stone, slopes, water, local paths and the Atlantic. La Palma, La Gomera and Gran Canaria are places, not anonymous dots. No tropical reef transplanted into Tenerife; no fish in air. An architectural fantasy can be extravagant while its site remains particular. Concepts and sketches are not surveyed engineering claims.

## Materials and colour

Use near-black text for readable print. Petrol blue and amber support the ocean, black and rust support the hillside section, vermilion and Prussian blue support flight. The common edition palette can include olive and cream without forcing every picture into brown wash. Uncoated stock supplies much of the physical warmth. Retain aged paper and the seam in the web viewer; do not bake shadows or fake folds into every print page.

Editorial assembly is legible through changes in type size, rules, cuttings, captions and visible corrections. Use those devices to signal a real change of voice or purpose. Avoid gratuitous tape, dirt, stains and random rotations across reading text. Each contributor still cares whether a reader can read. This is a gathering of different visual hands, not one simulated harried editor applying a finish to everything.

## Historical voices

Real figures are imagined contributors. Do not invent authentic quotations, attendance records or book-origin claims. The final notes distinguish the fiction and source the historical anchors. A brief Clarke encounter is appropriate: [Smithsonian's manuscript record](https://www.si.edu/object/archives/components/sova-nasm-2015-0010-ref1779) identifies *The View from Serendip* in his 1977 work. The new scene borrows no prose from the book. The fuller publication record can be checked in the [Smithsonian collection finding aid](https://sirismm.si.edu/EADpdfs/NASM.2015.0010.pdf).

Keep the attention distributed. Adding a famous guest should not push the local worker, the fisherman, the organiser or the women at the tables out of the picture. Do not add a biography whenever a passing conversation will do.

## Production and future revisions

The 70-page digest supersedes the 68-, 64-, 56- and 48-page interiors. The old 0.14-inch spine belongs to the 48-page order. A new printer-calculated spine is required before issuing another production cover; never scale the old wrap as a substitute. The front and back can still appear in a reading proof.

The older Letter edition is a preserved earlier layout, not an automatically updated equivalent. The new feature compositions are shared editorially with the website but drawn explicitly in the digest. The three-feature compositor is `print/digest-features.py`; the broader article/office pass is `print/digest-contributors.py`. Web compositions are in `magazine-features.js` and `contributor-pages.js`. The new inhabited spreads and Alexander sequence use `print/digest-worlds.py`; web counterparts use `world-pages.js` and `worlds.css`. Atlas, documents and notes still adapt the older renderer with revised headings. The cover is still the earlier design. This is a broader interior revision, not a claim that every page has been newly art-directed.

Review every changed facing spread at reading size, all pages as contact sheets, paragraph preservation, references, image resolution, font embedding, glyphs and safe margins. There must be no clipped captions or text overlaps. A content-complete PDF is not a successful design by itself. Inspect a physical copy at 100% before another run.

## Reference ladder for the contributor revision

The supplied examples span periods. The modern *Dazed* spread is evidence for asymmetry and an image crossing a gutter, not for 1977 typography. The two historic newspaper pages show density and competing small images; the displayed “1970” cover has not been authenticated. The BOHN advertisement belongs to an earlier industrial-future tradition. Do not label every supplied image a seventies original.

[MoMA's catalogue records *Fluxus cc V TRE Fluxus*, no. 2, in 1964](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/128175). [M+ holds *Archigram 1*, 1961](https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/collection/objects/magazine-archigram-1-ca36-t1/). These supply pre-1977 precedents for contributions that refuse a single polite visual voice. They do not justify copying their pages into ours.

[Sofia Oaks's Countere essay](https://www.countere.com/home/the-allure-of-retrofuturism) is a contemporary mood reference: it ranges across advertising, architecture and illustrated futures, and connects their appeal to earlier desires. It is not our chronological authority. Our emotional compass also allows grief, imperfection and the ridiculous, rather than a flawless technological utopia.

The [Science Museum's Times New Roman record](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8725748/0-2-inch-composition-punches-for-times-new-roman-typeface-series-number-327) dates its newspaper debut to 3 October 1932. Nimbus Roman is a digital substitute in that older typographic tradition, not an original period file.
