# Typeset as a magazine

Recorded October 27, 2026, after the first printed copies had been delivered.

The physical proof made several weaknesses plain: too much unused page, too
many returns to the cover picture, and type which did not fully belong to the
imagined object. Anselm pointed to Gertrude’s cream, brown, close-set display
lettering and rules, and asked for a stronger 1977 design pass.

Souvenir, Bookman, Avant Garde and the ITC world are useful period references.
The implemented type is a licensed digital revival of older designs: URW
Bookman for display, Nimbus Roman for long reading and Nimbus Sans for small
labels. Georgia and Arial no longer define the edition. Fraunces can evoke
the period but is a contemporary design; this pass chose older type models.

The web magazine now opens as real even/odd facing pages on wide screens,
with a fold between them, and single pages on phones. The viewer advances by
spreads on desktop. Both paper and interface use a quieter cream, chocolate,
rust and olive palette. Technical studies carry rules and captions rather
than modern cards.

The print edition contracts from 52 to 44 pages while retaining every essay.
Each editorial translation occupies one page; contents fit a two-column grid;
the delegate credential and cooperative certificate share a page. The booklet
now takes eleven tabloid sheets. A separate PDF supplies facing reader spreads.

The cover illustration appears once. A new shared-terrace painting, an evening
screening scene, and eleven original line studies replace repetition and empty
space. Eighteen brief workshop leaves give spare areas something specific to
hold. Women who are fictional participants make practical interventions about
school observation, nursery holidays, translation and disagreement.

Sheldon Renan and Andy Warhol appear briefly at the evening screening. This is
an invented passage through Tenerife, not a historical travel claim or a dated
account of their friendship. Renan’s 1975 television work supplies the historical
anchor. The final provenance notes distinguish the invented scene and artwork.

## Illustration directions

terrace

A single editorial architectural illustration for a serious international conference magazine printed in Tenerife in 1977. Landscape 3:2 composition. An inhabited communal hillside terrace on Tenerife: a shaded outdoor shared kitchen, modest whitewashed masonry, exposed dark volcanic stone, a timber workbench with a repaired chair, a few small people cooking and carrying baskets, narrow steps between self-built dwellings, glimpses of deep Atlantic blue. Focus on human scale practical daily use, no spectacular towers, no Teide mountain, no coverlike landscape. Hand drawn architectural perspective with fine brown pen and softly laid gouache washes, restrained flat color, burnt orange, honey ochre, olive green and muted ocean blue, natural warm offwhite paper. Credible late 1970s magazine reproduction, slightly imperfect ink edges, not photorealistic, not a slick modern rendering, not cartoon caricature, not hyperdetailed. No lettering, no text, no decorative border. A convincing full-page plate with editorial gravity and life.

screening

An editorial pen and gouache illustration for a 1977 international conference magazine, horizontal 3:2 composition. A small improvised evening 16mm film screening in a modest Tenerife conference room: reel-to-reel projector in foreground on a table with metal film cans, a single beam of light, folding chairs occupied by a varied group of women and men of different backgrounds, an open side doorway with a dark Atlantic evening outside. On the screen just abstract black and white experimental cinema shapes, no film title. Two small standing figures near back: one man with dark curly hair and glasses, one slim man with pale silver hair in a dark jacket. They are incidental within the room, no portraits or caricatures. Quiet conversational atmosphere before or after the screening. Historically plausible clothing and analogue equipment for 1977. Fine sepia pen crosshatching with sparse flat gouache: tobacco brown, rust, mustard, muted olive, cream paper. Warm earnest illustration from an old architectural magazine, not photorealistic, not glossy modern render, not cartoon. Leave breathing room and clear simple shapes, no typography or labels.

screeningRevision

Removed the geometrically incorrect visible projector beam; preserved the room, audience, screen, palette and drawing style.
