# The village that built us

The conference became interesting through the encounters it might have made possible. Real people enter an imagined gathering at a moment when something in their work is changing. Their historical work anchors the year; the attendance, conversations and Tenerife influences are fiction, explained at the end rather than interrupting the magazine.

Christopher Alexander’s four-page essay turns on a fisherman’s quiet guidance through Bocacangrejo. The village teaches through stairs, shared edges, white houses, effort and daily encounters. “What we build builds us in turn” is an invented line for this essay. His proposed machine evaluation of patterns remains tied to particular people and landscapes. The notes acknowledge the real 1970 book draft and 1979 publication rather than claiming Tenerife originated his thinking.
