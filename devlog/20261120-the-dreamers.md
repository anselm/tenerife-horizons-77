# We side with the dreamers

Anselm's editorial note, received 20 November 2026 during the first restoration of three individually designed magazine features. Preserved verbatim below as a source, not rewritten into magazine copy. The [editorial and design compass](20261120-editorial-and-design-compass.md) interprets its implications for the work.

> The personal stories of mine are fine to be woven in - I actually felt also like there was something profound about the pairwise friendships that seemed to surface of their own accord. This book ended up being a totem to those kinds of friendships.
>
> Also, on a personal note, there’s something very tongue-in-cheek in at least my fascination around retro-futurism. I’m reminded of Wes Anderson, Terry Gilliam, Miguel de Cervantes Saavedra - there’s a humor yes, but a pathos, a grief around mortality, the limits of life, our quixotic pursuits. That we are all chasing dreams that time will undo. We build these castles of identity; that we are chivalrous, that our goals are life-or-death - that there is meaning somehow despite ourselves, that magic is possible. In these futurist studies we inhabit the sincerity of the participants. These are worlds sprawling, grotesque, overflowing sweating sewage of excess; a Robert Crumb cartoon - curling around us - tentacles dripping with saliva. Worlds lacking control, boundaries, self, identity - and agents construct elaborate, fragile systems of meaning - drawing lines on chaos - attempting to impose our own curious Riemann Hypothesis on the unpredictable cacophony of noise around us… Ultimately I think we side with the dreamers, that their trust that magic, serendipity, fecund life, wins over brittle order.
